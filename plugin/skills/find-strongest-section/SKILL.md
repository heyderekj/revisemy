# Find the strongest section

This is the most useful move in homepage feedback. Almost every homepage already has one section that works: it's clear, it shows the product, and it could only belong to this product. Usually it's halfway down the page, and the hero above it is weaker. The fastest way to a better page is to use what's already working, not to start over.

## Hard rules

1. **Pick one.** Name a single strongest section. Two at most, and only if they're doing different jobs.
2. **Say why in concrete terms.** "It's clean" isn't a reason. "It shows the actual reader with a real passage, and you get it without reading the caption" is.
3. **Judge from the rendered page,** at 1440×900 and 390×844. Never from the source alone.
4. **Plan before building.** Don't change code unless the person asks for the move to be made.

## What makes a section strong

Score each section against these. The strongest one hits most of them.

| Signal | What it looks like |
| --- | --- |
| Shows, doesn't tell | You understand it with the text covered |
| Specific | Real product, real content, real names, not placeholders or stock |
| Clear | One idea, a heading that says it plainly |
| Distinctive | It couldn't be on a competitor's page |
| On brand | It uses the color, type or motif that makes the product recognizable |
| Answers "why should I care?" | Seeing it makes the value obvious |

Things that often turn out to be the strongest section: a day-by-day or step-by-step breakdown, a live slice of the product, a section with mixed media (traditional imagery next to UI), a strong illustration, and the app icon itself.

## The two moves

### Promote it to the hero

When the strongest section explains the product better than the current hero does.

- Move it up, and give it the plain heading and the primary button.
- Keep the old hero only if something in it is still needed. Often its best line becomes the supporting text.
- If the section relies on scroll animation, keep the content and drop most of the motion. A hero has to work in the first frame.

### Make it the template

When the hero is fine but the sections below it feel flat, or every section looks different.

- Pull out what makes it work — its visual language, its density, its balance of image and text — and rebuild the two or three weakest sections with the same approach.
- The hero can borrow its elements too. A hero built from the parts of the strongest section usually beats a hero on a background photo.

Many pages need both: promote it, then rebuild the rest in its image.

## Workflow

1. **Capture** the whole page at desktop and phone width, or work from the person's screenshots.
2. **List the sections** in order, one line each: what it's for and what it shows.
3. **Score** each against the table. Pick the strongest.
4. **Pick the move**: promote, template, or both.
5. **Name the two or three sections** that should be rebuilt in its image, and what each would become.
6. **Build it** only if asked. Follow `homepage-craft` while you do.

## Required output format

```
**Strongest section:** <name it so the person can find it>
Why it works: <two or three concrete reasons>

**The move:** <promote to hero / use as template / both>
<two or three lines on how>

**Rebuild in its image**
1. <section> → <what it becomes>
2. …

**Leave alone:** <sections that are fine as they are, if any>
```

## Tone

Encouraging, because the point of this skill is that the maker has already made something good. Be specific about what's good so they can do more of it.
