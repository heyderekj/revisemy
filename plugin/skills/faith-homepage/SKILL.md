# Faith homepage

These rules come from feedback on faith-app landing pages. Faith tools get the general things wrong that every homepage does (see `homepage-craft`), plus a handful of their own. This skill adds those. Load it on top of `homepage-craft` or `review-homepage`. It doesn't replace them.

## Hard rules

1. **Plain words, even for believers.** Your audience doesn't share one vocabulary across traditions, and plenty of visitors are new.
2. **Real faces, real churches.** Proof in this space is about trust and community, not logos and metrics.
3. **Reverent, not stock.** Imagery should feel true to a real congregation, a real study and a real home.
4. **No AI imagery of scripture, people or places of worship.** Wrong words in a verse or a generic church interior lose trust immediately.

## The rules

### Say what it is in words a newcomer knows

Terms that are obvious inside one tradition are opaque outside it. If someone might ask "what is this, and what am I supposed to do?", define it in the first frame.

- "Daily office", "lectio", "plant", "ChMS", "P-Day", "small group curriculum": use the plain version ("church plant", "daily prayer readings") or add a short line that explains it.
- For a practice the visitor may not know, a short first-visit intro (a line, a short dialog) helps, but don't make people get through it before they can start.

### Position as the best at your practice, not a social network

Prayer, study and community apps often drift into sounding like social media. That invites comparison with social media and raises the wrong worries.

- Say what you help people do: pray together, finish the Bible, study deeper, plan a service. Then say why you're better at it than the other apps for that practice.
- A "Why <name>" section that names the difference — how it's prayed, how it's read, who's on the other end — beats a feature list.
- If you're built for a platform churches already use (Planning Center and similar), lead with your own name and category. Otherwise you'll be known as "the app for Planning Center".

### Proof with faces and church names

- Testimonials with a photo, a name, a role and a church name. "Pastor, Grace Fellowship, Tulsa" beats an anonymous quote.
- For ministries and networks: the churches, people and places you've served, with numbers when you have them.
- Logos only when they're churches or organizations people know.

### Imagery that feels true

- Real congregations, real homes, real Bibles with notes in the margins, real hands. Not glossy stock of a generic church.
- Traditional media (art, manuscripts, paper, handwriting) next to clean UI can be beautiful and fitting, as long as it reads as one system.
- Parent and child, group and leader: show the relationship your product serves.

### Scripture with purpose

- One verse that explains why you made this belongs in the founder note or near the call to action. A verse on every section turns into wallpaper.
- Show scripture inside the product as the product shows it. It's the best demo you have.
- Use exact references and real text. Never generated.

### Your testimony, after the what

A short note on who made this and why is unusually powerful in this space. People want to support a fellow believer's work. Keep it after the visitor understands the product.

- For a network or ministry, focus less on your story and more on what the network does and how.

### Pricing and money, stated plainly

- Free, donation-based, per-church or per-seat: say it clearly and early. Churches budget carefully and distrust surprise costs.
- If it's free forever, say so in plain words, and say how it's sustained if people will wonder.

### Expect a range of tastes

- An all-dark theme is uncommon for church software and can feel cold to the audience. Explore a daylight version, especially if the app icon is light.
- Reverent doesn't mean dull. A strong brand with warmth and personality stands out in a space full of similar sites.

## Checklist

| Issue | Fix |
| --- | --- |
| Insider term in the hero | The plain version, or a short line that defines it |
| Sounds like a social network | Lead with the practice it helps; add a "Why <name>" section |
| Named after an integration | Your own name and category first |
| Anonymous quotes | Photo, name, role and church |
| Stock or AI church imagery | Real congregations, homes, Bibles and hands |
| Scripture as decoration | One verse with purpose, plus scripture shown inside the product |
| No founder note, or it's first | A short testimony, after how it works |
| Unclear cost | Free, donation or per-church, stated plainly and early |
| Cold all-dark theme | Explore a daylight theme from the app icon |
