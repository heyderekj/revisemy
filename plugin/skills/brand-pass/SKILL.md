# Brand pass

A lot of homepages have great bones and no personality. The product is good and the page is fine, but nothing about it would stick. Then people describe the product by what it connects to or by its category, not by its name. The fix is rarely a rebrand. It's usually one thing the maker already has, used with more confidence.

## Hard rules

1. **Start from what exists.** Find the brand element that already works before proposing anything new. Most pages have one.
2. **One system.** Every visual on the page should look like it came from the same hand. Random is worse than plain.
3. **Real over generated.** No AI images with readable text, wrong details or a generic sheen. They cost trust.
4. **Push, don't add.** Use the existing color, motif or type more boldly before introducing new ones.

## Find the element that works

Look for these, in roughly this order:

- **The app icon.** Often the most designed thing the maker owns. If it's strong, it can anchor the hero, set the palette and lend its shapes to the page.
- **A color.** One color used with conviction (backgrounds, sections, the icon) is a brand. One purple button on a white page is not yet.
- **A motif.** A shape in the logo — a slash, a flame, a curve — repeated subtly in dividers, crops or backgrounds.
- **A handmade touch.** Handwritten text, hand-drawn lines or a paper texture. Extend it to illustrations, annotations and underlines, alongside or instead of some photography.
- **The share image.** Sometimes the Open Graph image is the best brand expression the product has. Make the page live up to it.

## Checks

### Logo lockup

- The wordmark is sized with the icon: noticeably smaller than the icon's height, never matching it, never so small that it reads like a caption.
- The wordmark's weight holds next to the icon. A thin, delicate serif next to a bold mark looks like two brands.
- Get the basics right before a redesign: a polished mark and a clean wordmark.

### Type

- A display face with character, used at size. Avoid the faces so overused right now that they read as a template, and serifs too thin to hold at heading size.
- Fontshare is a good free library for replacing them.
- Two families at most.

### Palette and theme

- Use the brand color in big surfaces, not only buttons. Creams and neutrals can carry warmth too.
- A dark theme is a choice. If the audience expects daylight, explore a light theme drawn from the app icon. If staying dark, refine it: more contrast in type, color and illustration doing the lifting, less flat black.
- Add texture where a flat color is doing nothing, especially if the texture adds meaning.

### Imagery

- Photos, illustrations and UI share a treatment: crop, corner radius, shadow and color grade.
- Mixed media (traditional imagery next to UI) works when it's one deliberate system. A background photo, then a UI card, then an AI assistant panel isn't a system.
- No glow, gradients or effects on the mark unless they're part of the brand everywhere.

### Share-ready

- The Open Graph image, favicon and page title all carry the brand.

## Workflow

1. **Capture** the page at desktop and phone width, plus the Open Graph image and favicon.
2. **Name the strongest brand element** and why it works.
3. **Run the checks.** Collect issues.
4. **Propose how to carry the element through the page**: three to five specific places it should show up.
5. **Suggest two or three reference sites**, distinct from each other, from `homepage-craft/REFERENCES.md`.
6. **Build** only if asked.

## Required output format

```
**Brand element to build on:** <what> — <why it works>

**Carry it through**
1. <where> — <how>
…

**Fix**
1. <lockup / type / palette / imagery issue> — <change>
…

**Look at**
- <site> — <what to take from it>
```

## Tone

Enthusiastic about what's there. "Love the app icon" is a fine opening when it's true. Then be exact: name the size, the weight, the color and the place.
