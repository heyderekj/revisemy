# Cut the homepage

Most homepages have too much on them. Every section was added for a reason, and nobody went back to take any out. A simple idea deserves a simple page. Cutting is usually the fastest improvement a homepage can get, because whatever's left gets more attention.

## Hard rules

1. **Every cut says where the content goes:** deleted, moved to its own page, folded into a dialog, merged into another section, or moved later. Nothing valuable just disappears.
2. **Keep the strongest section.** Never cut the section that's doing the most work (see `find-strongest-section`), even if it's long.
3. **Order is part of the cut.** A section in the wrong place is as costly as an extra one.
4. **Plan before editing.** Don't change code unless asked.

## What to cut

Hunt for these. Each one has a usual destination.

| Pattern | Where it goes |
| --- | --- |
| A section that repeats one above it, in different words | Delete. Keep whichever shows more |
| The full feature list or "full platform" | Its own page. Keep three to six on the homepage as an icon grid |
| Side information in a second column next to the main point | A small link that opens a dialog |
| An eyebrow that repeats the heading | Delete, or fold its words into the heading |
| Cards with an icon and a paragraph each, covering everything | Delete most. Keep the few that matter as a grid of short lines |
| Two equal store buttons | One button that offers both |
| A name field, sign-up or multi-step onboarding before any value | Move after the first value. For email products, send the first email straight away |
| A hero video that has to be played to show anything | Replace with screenshots just below the button |
| A marquee or carousel of items moving past | A still grid or list |
| Scroll-jacked or scroll-triggered sections that hold content back | Keep the content, drop most of the motion |
| A decorative hero animation that pulls focus from the heading | Remove, or reduce it until the heading wins |
| A long founder story above the product | Shorten and move it after "how it works" |
| An odd sliver of a section between two dark ones, or above the footer | Make it a real section, or merge it |
| Background photos behind text that say nothing about the product | Remove; let the product or the strongest section carry the hero |

## The order

For most app and tool homepages, this order works:

1. **What it is**: plain heading, one line, primary button
2. **See it**: the product, right below
3. **How it works**: three or four steps
4. **Why this one**: what's different, not everything that's included
5. **Proof**: real people, real names, real numbers
6. **Your why**: a short founder note, if there is one
7. **Start**: the button again, plus pricing or FAQ if needed

A search-first product (a directory, a finder) collapses this to search and filters in the hero, a few example results, and a link to learn more.

## Workflow

1. **Capture** the whole page at desktop and phone width, or work from screenshots.
2. **List the sections** in order, with what each one is for.
3. **Mark each one**: keep, cut, move, merge or fold into a dialog, using the table.
4. **Propose the new order** using the sequence above.
5. **Edit** only if asked. Move content to its new page or dialog before deleting it from the homepage.

## Required output format

```
**Cut list**
| Section | Do | Goes to |
| --- | --- | --- |
| <name> | cut / move / merge / dialog / keep | <destination> |

**New order**
1. …

**Left alone:** <sections that stay as they are, and why>
```

## Tone

Decisive. Makers are attached to their sections, so be kind about it, but don't hedge: "Cut this. The section above already says it, and says it better."
